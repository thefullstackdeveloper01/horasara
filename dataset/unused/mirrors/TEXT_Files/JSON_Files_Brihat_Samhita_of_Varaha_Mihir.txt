    • scientific_approach: Varahamihira adhered to scientific principles in astrology, quoting ancient sages but stating his own confirmed decisions.
    • literary_merit: The text employs at least 63 different Sanskrit metres, with Arya being the most frequent.
    • influence: The text became more popular than earlier similar texts due to its comprehensiveness and lucidity.
    • combinatorics:

      • meaning: If there are 16 types of substances, choosing 4 at a time yields 1820 types of perfumes.
    • geographical_content: Chapter 14 describes ancient India divided into 9 regions based on asterisms, taken from Parasara Tantra.

    • arboriculture: Chapter 55 (Vṛkṣāyurveda) covers tree planting, care, diseases, and fertilizers.
  • historical_context:
    • author_background: Varahamihira was the son of Adityadasa of Ujjain and was ranked among the nine gems of Vikramaditya's court.
    • date_debate: Scholars place Varahamihira in the 5th-6th century CE. Al-Biruni confirms 505 CE as the date of his Pancha Siddhantika.
    • contemporaries: He was a contemporary of the neo-Kalidasa and Amarasimha.
    • source_material: He drew from earlier Samhitas by Parasara, Garga, and others, acknowledging their contributions.
